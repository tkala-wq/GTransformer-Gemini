# 02 · Approved Sources

**Topic:** The only sources allowed for risk ratings and claim checks, their URLs, and which source wins when they conflict.
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-14

> **Strict rule:** cite every risk claim. If no approved source covers it, write *"No authoritative data found."* Never fill the gap with blogs, brand websites, retailer pages or AI-generated text.

---

## 1. Trust tiers

| Tier | Role | Can it set a risk rating? |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1: Regulators** | Laws, bans, limits, recalls, import alerts and certifications in the market of sale | ✅ Yes, decisive |
| **Tier 2: Scientific and international bodies** | Hazard assessments, intake limits and international standards | ✅ Yes, supports or sets a rating |
| **Tier 3: Reputable journalism** | Background and context only | ❌ Never |

## 6. When sources conflict

Apply these rules in order:

1. **Product-specific action beats general hazard data.** A recall, import alert or warning letter naming this product decides the matter.
2. **The law in the market of sale decides legal status.** For a product sold at Whole Foods, the FDA or USDA rule is reported as the legal status. For a product sold in India, FSSAI (or CDSCO or AYUSH, once approved) is.
3. **The stricter credible assessment decides the risk tier.** If the FDA permits an ingredient but EFSA or the EU bans it, the tier is at least **Moderate**, and the report states both: *"Permitted in the US (FDA); banned in EU cosmetics (EU Commission)."*
4. **Newer beats older** within the same body (e.g. EFSA's 2023 BPA opinion replaces its 2015 one).
5. **Risk assessment beats hazard classification.** An IARC class alone describes hazard; note the exposure route.
6. **Tier 3 never breaks a tie.**

### Citation format
`[Body] — [Document or page title] ([year or date accessed]) — URL`
Example: `FDA — 21 CFR 700.13, Use of mercury compounds in cosmetics — https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-700.13`

(eCFR at https://www.ecfr.gov is the official US government publication of the CFR and counts as Tier 1 when citing FDA or USDA rules.)

## 7. Product-information websites (US sale websites only)

> **Constraint (Problem Statement, Guardrails):** online product lookups use **only websites that sell the product in the United States**.

These websites are used **only to identify a product and read its label**: name, net quantity, price, ingredient list, claims and images. They are **never** a source for a risk rating, which comes from Tiers 1–2 above.

| Allowed (sell in the US) | Examples |
|---|---|
| The brand's US website | headandshoulders.com/en-us, srisritattvausa.com |
| US grocery and retail | wholefoodsmarket.com, amazon.com, walmart.com, target.com |
| US pharmacies | cvs.com, walgreens.com |

| Not allowed (sell outside the US) | Examples |
|---|---|
| A brand's non-US website | srisritattva.com (India), headandshoulders.ca |
| Non-US retailers and pharmacies | bigbasket.com, 1mg.com, apollopharmacy.in, flipkart.com, nykaa.com, amazon.in |

### Designated product pages
Pages named by the project owner. These are the **primary web label** for that product and take precedence over other US listings (rule 4 below).

| Product | Designated page | Added |
|---|---|---|
| Sri Sri Tattva Protein Shampoo (200 mL) | https://srisritattvausa.com/products/protein-shampoo?_pos=1&_psq=protein+sham&_psid=ff0d59a8f&_ss=e | 2026-11-14 |

**Rules**
1. If the only page for a product is on a non-US site, **don't use it**. Say: *"This product isn't listed on a US sale website."*
2. Prices are taken in **USD** from the US listing, with the date.
3. Official label registries are **not** sale websites and stay allowed: DailyMed (NIH) for OTC drug labels, and the NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database.
4. If two US listings disagree, prefer the designated product page, then the brand's US site, then the listing with a back-label image. Note the difference.
