# 03 · Key-Terms Registry

**Topic:** A running glossary of label terms: the legal definition, the certifying body, and red flags that the term is being misused.
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-14
**How to use:** check every front-of-pack and back-of-pack claim against this list, then assign a Claim Integrity status (Verified / Consistent / Unverifiable / Misleading / False / Missing required statement) as defined in `01-scoring-methodology.md`.

> **No legal definition = Unverifiable**, unless an approved registry or certificate confirms it.

---

## A. Organic and agriculture

### Organic (US)
- **Legal definition:** USDA National Organic Program, 7 CFR Part 205. Labeling categories (7 CFR 205.301):
  - **"100% organic"**: all organic ingredients. May use the USDA seal.
  - **"Organic"**: ≥95% organic ingredients (excluding water and salt). May use the USDA seal.
  - **"Made with organic [ingredients]"**: ≥70% organic. **No USDA seal.**
  - **<70%**: may only name organic ingredients in the ingredient list.
- **Personal care:** the FDA does not define "organic" for cosmetics. A shampoo or soap may carry the USDA seal only if its agricultural ingredients meet NOP standards and it is certified.
- **Certifier:** a USDA-accredited certifying agent. The certifier's name must appear below the producer's name ("Certified organic by …").
- **Verify:** USDA Organic Integrity Database (see `05`).
- **Red flags:** the seal on a "made with organic" product; no certifier named; operation not found or listed as *Suspended/Revoked/Surrendered*; "organic" in the brand name of an uncertified product; a single organic ingredient used to imply the whole product is organic.

### Organic (India)
- **Legal definition:** FSSAI Food Safety and Standards (Organic Foods) Regulations, 2017. Food must be certified under **NPOP** (APEDA) or **PGS-India**.
- **Mark:** **Jaivik Bharat** logo, plus the NPOP India Organic logo or the PGS-India logo.
- **Verify:** Jaivik Bharat portal; FSSAI licence on FoSCoS.
- **Red flags:** "organic" with no Jaivik Bharat logo on food; PGS-India products (meant for domestic direct sale) presented as export-grade; organic claims on cosmetics that cite no certifier.

### Non-GMO / GMO-free
- **Legal definition:** **none** in US federal law. The USDA's **National Bioengineered Food Disclosure Standard** (7 CFR Part 66, mandatory since 1 Jan 2022) governs the **"bioengineered"** disclosure, not "non-GMO". Highly refined ingredients with no detectable modified genetic material are exempt from disclosure.
- **Certifier:** private, e.g. the Non-GMO Project (*not an approved source*; record it as an Unverifiable claim). USDA Organic rules prohibit genetic engineering, so certified-organic products are non-GMO by standard.
- **Red flags:** "GMO-free" or "non-GMO" on products that have no GMO version on the market (e.g. salt, water); a "bioengineered" disclosure on the same pack.

### Bioengineered (US)
- **Legal definition:** 7 CFR Part 66. Disclosed as text, the BE symbol, a QR code or a text-message option.
- **Red flag:** a QR-code-only disclosure with no phone number to text, or a missing disclosure on foods made with listed bioengineered crops.

### USDA "Natural" (meat and poultry only)
- **Legal definition:** USDA FSIS: no artificial ingredients and minimally processed. Does **not** apply to supplements or cosmetics.

## B. "Natural" and "clean" language

### Natural (food or supplements)
- **Legal definition:** **none formally.** FDA's long-standing policy is that "natural" means nothing artificial or synthetic (including colour additives from any source) has been added that would not normally be expected in the food.
- **Red flags:** artificial or "nature-identical" flavours; synthetic preservatives; "natural flavours" used to imply the whole product is natural.

### Natural (cosmetics)
- **Legal definition:** **none** (FDA).
- **Status:** Unverifiable by default. **Misleading** if synthetic preservatives, fragrance or ethoxylated surfactants make up a major part of the formula.

### Clean label / clean beauty
- **Legal definition:** none. Marketing term only.
- **Status:** Unverifiable. Look at the actual ingredients.

### Chemical-free
- **Legal definition:** none, and impossible: every substance, water included, is a chemical.
- **Status:** **Misleading**.

### Non-toxic
- **Legal definition:** none for cosmetics or supplements.
- **Status:** Unverifiable, or Misleading if a High-tier ingredient is present.

### Plant-based / herbal
- **Legal definition:** none.
- **Red flags:** a plant extract listed near the end of the list (below 1%) while the claim dominates the front of the pack.

## C. "Free-from" claims

| Term | Legal definition | Status rule | Red flags |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paraben-free** | None | Consistent if no "-paraben" ingredient | Swapped for MI/MCI or formaldehyde releasers (see `04`) |
| **Sulfate-free** | None | Consistent if no "sulfate" ingredient | Similar harsh surfactants such as sulfonates; "sulfate-free" while SLES is listed is **False** |
| **Phthalate-free** | None | Unverifiable if "fragrance/parfum" is listed (fragrance mixtures need not disclose components) | "Fragrance" on the list |
| **BPA-free** | None. FDA has prohibited BPA in baby bottles and sippy cups (2012) and in infant-formula packaging coatings (2013) | Unverifiable unless the maker documents it | A substitute bisphenol (BPS, BPF) may be used instead |
| **Fragrance-free** | None (FDA) | Consistent if no fragrance or parfum | Masking fragrance; essential oils listed |
| **Unscented** | None | Note: may contain masking fragrance | Treat differently from "fragrance-free" |
| **Gluten-free** (food, US) | **FDA 21 CFR 101.91**: <20 ppm gluten | Consistent if no gluten grains; Verified only with a test certificate | Wheat, barley, rye or malt on the list → **False** |
| **Sugar-free** (food, US) | **FDA 21 CFR 101.60(c)**: <0.5 g sugars per serving (RACC) | Check the panel | Sugar alcohols or high-intensity sweeteners hidden |
| **No added sugar** (US) | FDA 21 CFR 101.60(c)(2): conditions apply | Check the ingredient list for syrups and juice concentrates | Fruit-juice concentrate used as a sweetener |
| **Microbead-free** | US Microbead-Free Waters Act 2015 bans plastic microbeads in rinse-off cosmetics | Consistent | Polyethylene or polypropylene beads listed |
| **Cruelty-free** | None in the US. India prohibits animal testing of cosmetics (2014) and the import of animal-tested cosmetics | Unverifiable (private certifiers such as Leaping Bunny are not approved sources) | — |
| **Vegan** | None | Consistent if no animal-derived ingredient | Keratin, collagen, lanolin, beeswax, carmine, milk proteins |

## D. Nutrient and health claims (supplements and food)

### High protein / excellent source of protein (US)
- **Legal definition:** 21 CFR 101.54: **"high"/"excellent source"** ≥20% DV per RACC; **"good source"** 10–19% DV. %DV for protein must be corrected for protein quality (PDCAAS).
- **Red flag:** the protein grams on the front don't match the Supplement or Nutrition Facts panel.

### Healthy (US)
- **Legal definition:** FDA final rule updating "healthy" (published Dec 2024; compliance date Feb 2028). It requires food-group content and limits on added sugars, sodium and saturated fat.
- **Red flag:** "healthy" on a product with high added sugar or sodium.

### Low sodium (US)
- **Legal definition:** 21 CFR 101.61: ≤140 mg per RACC.

### Structure/function claims (US supplements)
- **Legal definition:** DSHEA; 21 CFR 101.93. Claims like "supports muscle growth" are allowed **only** with this disclaimer:
  *"These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."*
- **Status:** missing disclaimer → **Missing required statement**. A disease claim ("cures hair loss", "treats arthritis") on a supplement → **False** (unapproved drug claim).

### Health supplement / nutraceutical (India)
- **Legal definition:** FSSAI Food Safety and Standards (Health Supplements, Nutraceuticals, Food for Special Dietary Use, Food for Special Medical Purpose, and Prebiotic and Probiotic Food) Regulations, 2016, as amended in 2022. The label must carry **"NOT FOR MEDICINAL USE"**.
- **Red flags:** disease-cure claims; no FSSAI licence number.

### "Clinically proven"
- **Legal definition:** none.
- **Status:** Unverifiable unless a study is cited that can be traced. For OTC drugs, the efficacy of a monograph active at an approved strength is established by the FDA monograph, so "controls dandruff" is Consistent, but "clinically proven" is still Unverifiable.

### "Heavy-metal tested" / "third-party tested"
- **Legal definition:** none. US supplements have no federal heavy-metal limit.
- **Status:** Unverifiable unless the test results (lab, batch, limits) are published.

### "No micronutrients"
- *(Listed as written in the problem statement.)* **Legal definition:** none, and the meaning is unclear (it may intend "no added micronutrients" or "no microplastics").
- **Status:** Unverifiable. Report it as an ambiguous claim and ask the maker what it means.

## E. Cosmetic and drug terms

| Term | Legal definition | Notes and red flags |
|---|---|---|
| **Hypoallergenic** | None (FDA) | Unverifiable. Fragrance or MI listed → Misleading |
| **Dermatologically tested / dermatologist recommended** | None | Unverifiable. "Tested" doesn't say what the result was |
| **pH balanced** | None | Unverifiable unless a pH value is given |
| **Anti-dandruff** (US) | An OTC drug claim. The product must contain a monograph active at an approved level: pyrithione zinc, selenium sulfide, coal tar, salicylic acid or sulfur (21 CFR 358.710); ketoconazole is approved separately | A "Drug Facts" box is required. Anti-dandruff claim with no active → **False** |
| **Ayurvedic** (US) | None. In the US, a product sold as Ayurvedic is regulated as a cosmetic, drug or dietary supplement according to its intended use and claims (FDA) | Unverifiable. Drug-like claims ("treats hair loss") → Misleading or False |
| **Ayurvedic** (India) | Ayurvedic drugs must be manufactured under a licence from the State Licensing Authority (Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940/Rules 1945; AYUSH) | A licence number is required on the label; no licence → Unverifiable |
| **Medicated** | Implies a drug | Must carry drug labeling, or else Misleading |

## F. Environmental and packaging claims

> FTC Green Guides (16 CFR Part 260) are the main US rule here. They are a *proposed* source (see `02`); until approved, record these claims as Unverifiable unless a Tier 1 registry confirms them.

| Term | Guide | Red flags |
|---|---|---|
| **Eco-friendly / green** | Broad, unqualified environmental claims are discouraged | No specific benefit stated |
| **Biodegradable** | Should break down within a reasonably short time in customary disposal | Plastic packs labelled "biodegradable" |
| **Recyclable** | Recycling facilities should be available to a substantial majority of consumers | Multi-layer sachets, pumps |
| **Plastic-free** | No legal definition | Plastic-lined cartons, plastic pumps |
